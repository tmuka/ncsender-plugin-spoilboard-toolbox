Setup

Describe your spoilboard’s grid once: dog holes, or lines lasered or engraved on it (set Hole Diameter to the line’s width). The machine’s travel comes from grblHAL.

List the holes once (locating holes, fixture pins, a custom layout), from a reference point you choose, usually the first hole. The machine’s travel comes from grblHAL.

With a touch probe (a 3D probe that triggers when its tip is pushed sideways; wood doesn’t conduct, so a plain pin can’t) in the spindle and connected: jog its tip into a hole, below the spoilboard’s top and roughly centered, and press Probe This Hole. It touches the hole’s sides in X and Y at the Z0 macros’ Probe Feed and finds its center. Or let it do the suggested holes: jog the tip to 1 to 4 mm above the spoilboard and hold Probe Suggested Holes. It goes to each at Safe Z, 6 mm down into it (stopping if it touches the spoilboard’s top instead), and centers itself. Not yet tried with a real 3D probe: stay by the stop button the first time, and please tell us how it went on GitHub.

By eye, with no probe: put a pin, a dowel or a V-bit in the spindle, jog it (keyboard, gamepad or pendant) down into the hole’s mouth until it’s centered, with an even gap all round, and press Centered by Eye. It records where the bit is, without moving; a pin that just fits the hole centers itself.

Measure two or more holes far apart (along a row and up a column) to fit the spacing and insets, and how far the spoilboard is turned (Turn); Apply waits for the second.

    Probe your spoilboard once. After that, with any measured tool, one macro puts work Z0 on the spoilboard or on top of your stock, with no probing, and another puts your old Z0 back. For machines that measure tool lengths (a tool setter or an ATC) running grblHAL. Midpoint macros for X/Y are extras.

    1Check the controller

    Asks the controller for its state ($G, $#) and tries one harmless expression. Nothing moves.

      2Settings

      From ncSender’s Standard Block probe settings.

      The spoilboard height, your saved Z0 and the stock thickness are kept in three spare work offsets' Z, which the controller keeps through power cycles; pick ones you don't use. The stock-top macro uses whatever thickness is stored: press Store for a known thickness, or run the measure macro. The macros switch to mm (G21) while they work, because grblHAL reports stored offsets and tool lengths in mm whatever the units mode.

      3Extras

      Midpoint moves first lift to ncSender’s Safe Z. They use the active work offset.

      4Macros

      The spoilboard and stock-top macros save the current Z0 first, unless it's already one of theirs, so back to saved returns to your probed Z0 however many times you switch.

      Holes

      Cut the T-tracks

      Just the T-track slots, no holes: for a sacrificial layer laid over a lower one that keeps the tracks and threaded inserts, so the top can be surfaced off and replaced without touching them. A slot along each of Setup’s T-tracks, through the top layer (Depth blank: the Spoilboard Thickness above, which must be the top layer’s alone) so the tracks below show, or a shallower dado to set new track in. Then cut the holes above, through the same layer, over the inserts. Z0 on the top layer’s top; it uses the bit, feeds and spindle above.

      Dowel fit test

      Before cutting them all, find the size your dowels fit: a row of test holes in scrap, smallest on the left. Put X0 Y0 on the scrap’s front left corner and Z0 on its top. It uses the bit, feeds and spindle above.

      Chamfer the holes

      With a V-bit, after cutting: a small bevel round each hole’s top edge, so dowels start easily and the edge doesn’t crumble. The same holes as above; Z0 on the spoilboard’s top.

      Grid lines

      No holes, just lines: a line along every row and column of Setup’s grids, crossing where the holes would be, with a laser or a V-bit. They stop short of keepout zones and break at T-tracks (Track Width from the T-tracks section). Then set Hole Diameter to the line’s width and zero on the crossings. Z0 on the spoilboard’s top (for a laser: focused on it). Not sure of the laser’s power and speed? Run the Laser test first.

      Laser test

      Find the laser’s power and speed for crisp lines: a small cross for every power and feed, on scrap of the same material. X0 Y0 on the scrap’s front left corner, the laser focused on its top. Each row is labelled with its feed, each column with its power; use the best for Grid lines and Labels.

      Engrave the labels

      The fixture table’s names beside the holes, with a V-bit or a laser, so the spoilboard reads like the map. Z0 on the spoilboard’s top (for a laser: focused on it).

      Holes through the stock, right over spoilboard holes: bolt holes to hold it down on threaded inserts, flip pins so a stock turned over for its second side drops back exactly where it was, or a fixture plate that sits on pins and bolts to inserts between them. Tick Stock in the XY0 Picker (size and thickness), clamp it some other way while it drills, and put Z0 on its top.

      Holes through the stock

      Drill the stock right over spoilboard holes: for bolts into threaded inserts below, for flip pins, so a stock flipped for its second side drops back exactly where it was, or for a fixture plate: two pins in dog holes, bolts into an insert grid between them, and a corner stop. The stock is the XY0 Picker’s (tick Stock, with its size and thickness) and the holes come from the grid picked in Setup. Clamp the stock down some other way first (it lifts to Safe Z between holes, over the clamps), and put Z0 on its top. Threaded inserts must sit at least Break Through below the spoilboard’s surface. It uses the bit, feeds and spindle above.

      Welcome to Spoilboard Toolbox

      Your spoilboard’s holes are already a precision fixture. This turns any of them into X0 Y0 in two taps, with no probe: jog roughly near a hole, tap Pick Nearest Hole, hold Set.

      Which is your spoilboard?

      No holes yet

      Lay out a new board and cut it. The board and the map come from the same numbers, so they agree from the first hole.

      1. Surface the board (QuickCut) and put Z0 on its top.
      2. Describe the grid you want in Setup, or load a preset.
      3. Spoilboard › Fit test in scrap, to choose the hole size.
      4. Spoilboard › Dog holes › Generate, check it in the visualizer and run it.

      Holes already there

      Map the holes you have, then zero on them every job.

      1. Type the hole size and spacing in Setup, or load a preset.
      2. Pick a hole, jog over its real center, press Grab … Center.
      3. Measure Holes on the three it suggests (a pin by eye, or a 3D probe), then Apply.
      4. Check with Go to Selected over a far hole, and save a profile.
