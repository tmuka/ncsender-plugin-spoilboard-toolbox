Setup

Describe your spoilboard’s grid once: dog holes, or lines lasered or engraved on it (set Hole Diameter to the line’s width). The machine’s travel comes from grblHAL.

List the holes once (locating holes, fixture pins, a custom layout), from a reference point you choose, usually the first hole. The machine’s travel comes from grblHAL.

With a touch probe (a 3D probe that triggers when its tip is pushed sideways; wood doesn’t conduct, so a plain pin can’t) in the spindle and connected: jog its tip into a hole, below the spoilboard’s top and roughly centered, and press Probe This Hole. It touches the hole’s sides in X and Y at the Z0 macros’ Probe Feed and finds its center. Probe two or more holes far apart (along a row and up a column) to fit the spacing and insets, and how far the spoilboard is turned; one fits the insets only.

    Probe your spoilboard once. After that, with any measured tool, one macro puts work Z0 on the spoilboard or on top of your stock, with no probing, and another puts your old Z0 back. For machines that measure tool lengths (a tool setter or an ATC) running grblHAL. Midpoint macros for X/Y are extras.

    The numbers come from the controller ($#). Refresh

    1Check the controller

    Asks the controller for its state ($G, $#) and tries one harmless expression. Nothing moves.

      2Settings

      From ncSender’s Standard Block probe settings.

      The spoilboard height, your saved Z0 and the stock thickness are kept in three spare work offsets' Z, which the controller keeps through power cycles; pick ones you don't use. The stock-top macro uses whatever thickness is stored: press Store for a known thickness, or run the measure macro. The macros switch to mm (G21) while they work, because grblHAL reports stored offsets and tool lengths in mm whatever the units mode.

      3Extras

      Midpoint moves first lift to ncSender’s Safe Z. They use the active work offset.

      4Macros

      The spoilboard and stock-top macros save the current Z0 first, unless it's already one of theirs, so back to saved returns to your probed Z0 however many times you switch.

      5Try it once

      1. Set the probe block on the spoilboard and load a tool whose length offset is measured and active.
      2. Jog the bit just above the block. In ncSender’s Macros tab, run . It probes down, stores the spoilboard height, and backs off.
      3. Without changing tools, run . It saves your current Z0, then puts Z0 on the spoilboard.
      4. Jog down onto the block with a paper feeler. Work Z should read about the block thickness. Or check it against ncSender’s own probe: run Probe → Standard Block → Z with the block still on the spoilboard, then again; work Z shouldn’t change by more than a few hundredths. If they don’t agree, don’t use the macros until they do.
      5. Run . Work Z0 goes back to where it was before step 3.

      7. For , first press Store for a known thickness, or put the block on the stock and run .

      The macros stop without changing anything when the machine isn’t homed, no tool length offset is active, or the spoilboard height (or stock thickness) hasn’t been stored yet. ncSender’s terminal shows error:72; each stop line in the macro has a comment saying what it checks.

      Re-run the record macro after surfacing the spoilboard (e.g. QuickCut’s Wasteboard Surfacing), or if your reference tool changes: the stored height is only as current as the last recording.

      Cut the dog holes you set up in the XY0 Picker, so the spoilboard and the map come from the same numbers.       Holes through the stock

      Drill the stock right over spoilboard holes: for bolts into threaded inserts below, or for flip pins, so a stock flipped for its second side drops back exactly where it was. The stock is the XY0 Picker’s (tick Stock, with its size and thickness) and the holes come from the grid picked in Setup. Clamp the stock down some other way first, and put Z0 on its top. It uses the bit, feeds and spindle above.

      Dowel fit test

      Before cutting them all, find the size your dowels fit: a row of test holes in scrap, smallest on the left. Put X0 Y0 on the scrap’s front left corner and Z0 on its top. It uses the bit, feeds and spindle above.

      Chamfer the holes

      With a V-bit, after cutting: a small bevel round each hole’s top edge, so dowels start easily and the edge doesn’t crumble. The same holes as above; Z0 on the spoilboard’s top.

      Engrave the labels

      The fixture table’s names beside the holes, with a V-bit or a laser, so the spoilboard reads like the map. Z0 on the spoilboard’s top (for a laser: focused on it).
